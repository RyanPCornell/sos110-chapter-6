The two figures above are rough translations, not measured area. See Methodology.
This tool is for teaching, not measurement. It estimates only three household streams (driving, home electricity, and food protein) using published average factors, so it is not a complete Ecological Footprint and not a measurement of any individual's actual impact. Real footprints also include flights, heating fuel, goods, services, housing, water, and public infrastructure. Averages hide wide variation: the same food can differ fiftyfold between producers. Treat the total as an order of magnitude for comparing choices, not a precise number.

The global-hectare figure is an approximation

The Ecological Footprint is measured in global hectares, and its carbon component is the forest area needed to absorb CO2. Global Footprint Network does not publish a single public conversion rate, and the values in circulation disagree, so the gha and Earths figures here use a sequestration rate of about 1.8 tonnes of CO2 per global hectare per year and a per-person biocapacity of 1.5 global hectares. Both are shown as secondary, rounded numbers for a sense of scale. Anyone needing a defensible Footprint should use Global Footprint Network's own accounts [5], not this tool.

What is deliberately excluded

Flights, natural gas and heating oil, water, goods, clothing, electronics, construction, public services, and the food beyond the protein servings above. Each is real and several are large. A calculator that asked for everything would need assumptions this class has no way to check, so the scope is kept to streams with strong public factors.

Averages, not your grid or your farm

The electricity factor is a national average: a kWh in a coal-heavy region carries far more carbon than one in a hydro-heavy region. The driving factor assumes an average gasoline car, so an electric vehicle or a motorcycle is misrepresented. Food figures are global production averages and exclude cooking and household waste.
